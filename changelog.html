Changelog

Every release, newest first. Security marks what every device should get at once. A Major update (in bold at
the start of an entry, and in the file MAJOR of the repository) changes how a session connects or runs: a device
with a management folder installs it only once the manager released it, one without asks on its screen.
Downloads and signed update packages: releases.

0.2.27 (2026-10-28)

  • The anonymous counts get out (#167): a device started
    them in the background of its report, which runs as a oneshot service, and systemd stopped them when the report
    ended, before anything was sent, on every device. They now run in a unit of their own (systemd-run): seen on an
    OptiPlex 3000 Thin Client, they reach the counter within a minute. What is sent does not change
    (privacy), and ANON_STATS=off still turns them off.
  • Manager: no more -EncodedCommand: the launcher started Windows PowerShell with the manager as a Base64 command
    line, which EDRs such as SentinelOne report as suspicious. It now starts OpenThinRDP-Manager.start.ps1, a fixed
    script of a few lines signed with the release, and hands it the manager on its standard input: nothing of the
    manager goes on a command line or to the disk. After the update the manager starts once more through the launcher
    of before; from the next start on, through the new one.
  • Manager: the site key reaches every device by itself: a device that was off when the site key was sent, or was
    installed again, took no update from the folder any more (UPDATE_KEY=site: "invalid signature"). The manager, open
    with the vault, now sends the key to such a device as soon as it is on (SSH), and notes it in changes.log.
  • Manager: it opens again after updating itself: after an update of 0.2.24-0.2.26 it could close instead of
    starting again (the new start found the one still closing and gave way to it). It now waits for it.

0.2.26 (2026-10-26)

  • Webcams that keep up (#165): a webcam that compresses
    to H.264 by itself sent that H.264 to Windows as it was, and those frames cannot be dropped: a late one held the
    next and the picture fell behind. The webcam is now read in MJPEG and encoded by the device, which drops a late
    frame and keeps up, on the video engine of the processor (Intel Quick Sync, VA-API) so the processor stays
    free: 30 fps at 1080p on an OptiPlex 3000 Thin Client, the processor 89% idle. And Windows is offered at most
    1080p (720p on a device without such a video engine), where it picked the largest a 2K webcam had. Three
    settings change it: CAMERA_H264, CAMERA_HWACCEL, CAMERA_MAX, in the manager too.
  • FreeRDP fb0198dbe: the options of the
    camera channel (h264, hwaccel, maxres), built with VA-API.
  • Tip: in a dark room many webcams lengthen their exposure and give half the frames (10 fps instead of 20-30). More
    light, or a webcam with a fixed frame rate, helps more than any setting.

0.2.25 (2026-10-26)

  • The manager is signed with Authenticode: OpenThinRDP-Manager.exe carries the signature of the project
    (CN=OpenThinRDP Project, with a timestamp). An EDR or antivirus that held the manager back (SentinelOne,
    #133) is told once to allow it by signer, and every next
    release passes: how. Your own
    code signing certificate still works too (Settings > Security > Code signing).
  • The right time after the change of the clock: Windows takes the time zone of the device only when a session
    opens, so a session open across the change to or from daylight saving time showed the time one hour off until it
    was opened again. The device now opens it again by itself when the clock changes: a few seconds, the programs stay
    open.
  • A new release is said where you look: Home lists New version for the devices (or of the manager) with
    what the folder publishes now, and the menu counts it on Updates. In 0.2.24 the window checked GitHub but showed
    it nowhere.
  • Readings that end: the iDRACs are read in parallel, firmware, memory modules and processors only every 6
    hours, and the next reading starts 3 minutes after the end of the last one (a reading longer than 3 minutes ran
    back to back). Each step and each part of a controller keeps how long it took.
  • Rings in proportion: memory, disks and the other rings of the manager were drawn full or empty, never in between.
  • A narrow window puts the cards of a row two by two, then one under the other, instead of a scroll to the side.
  • Charts keep their words straight (they were stretched with the drawing), the days of the scheduled jobs come in
    the language of the manager, and the hints that still named the settings of 0.2.23 lead to the line of the server.

0.2.24 (2026-10-26)

  • A new window for the manager: every page and every dialog drawn on the WebView2 of Windows (the installer of
    Microsoft comes with the manager when a PC lacks it). Columns you can widen and sort, filters, a right click menu
    on each line, a page that stays as you left it when you come back, one copy open at a time, and a start in a
    fraction of a second.
  • Settings in one place: Site first (time zone, language and keyboard of all the devices, language of the
    manager), then Infrastructure and servers, where each iDRAC, Proxmox, vCenter or Veeam server has its own account
    and a Test button that says in plain words what is wrong. No setting is written in two places any more.
  • The hardware of the servers as the iDRAC shows it: what the iDRAC says of each part (processors, memory, disks
    and RAID, power supplies, fans, temperatures, voltages, batteries, chassis intrusion, licences, log), then every
    disk with its remaining life and predicted failure, RAID volumes and the controller battery, memory module by
    module, processors, network ports, temperatures with their thresholds, the hardware log of 30 days and every
    firmware version. One line per server in Home.
  • Ignore a notice for a while, or until you restore it, with a note of why; it comes back by itself if it gets
    worse.
  • Who opens the manager: personal access with the Windows account (a user or an AD group, at their level),
    the access code kept for emergencies.
  • A USB device as a drive, also when the others go whole: USB_LOCAL (VID:PID) keeps it on the device in every
    mode, its storage a drive in Windows; the manager sets it, or USB_REDIRECT, from the peripherals of a device, for
    that device, its group or all, and plugs a device in again from there.
  • The time zone from a list in the settings of the device: every zone of tzdata, with a search while typing
    ("rome" finds Europe/Rome) instead of the exact name to type (#154).
  • Anonymous counts from the devices really leave now: they were sent with curl, which is not in the image, so
    no device ever sent one; now python3, already in the image, sends them. A new device sends its first counts with
    its own settings, before it takes the central ones; ANON_STATS=off in the central settings holds from then on,
    also after an update (privacy).

0.2.23 (2026-10-25)

  • The manager is a project of its own: openthinrdp-manager,
    free for any use, also commercial, and closed from this release. What it runs on your servers stays readable
    (commands\, rds\), and it shows what it will do before you confirm. The manager of the releases up to 0.2.22
    stays under the GNU GPL. The thin client does not change: GPLv3, open.
  • It works without devices too: the first start offers the servers alone, a Health Center for Hyper-V, clusters,
    Remote Desktop, SQL and backups.
  • A new Home: the infrastructure, the devices, one list of what to look at, the sign-ins of today in their phases,
    the jobs of today, 30 days of memory with a forecast. It shows the last reading of your PC at once, with its time,
    while the new one comes (the infrastructure takes about half a minute).
  • Experience: how long each sign-in takes (broker, VM, Windows), the latency of the sessions, dropped and frozen
    ones, per user and device, kept 40 days. Tell your users: see privacy.
  • Scheduled jobs: restart, switch off, a message or an update, once, daily or weekly, on devices or groups; each
    device reports how it went.
  • Certificates and the enterprise CA: every certificate of every server on its page (what uses it, when it
    expires, whether it renews by itself), the CA and its revocation list, and which RDP servers present a certificate a
    device could verify. Security > Enterprise CA requests the signing certificate of the manager from your CA and
    follows its renewals, sends the CA to the devices, which trust it from their next report, and gives the desktops
    their RDP certificate from your CA (a template and the GPO, renewed by themselves): the way to RDP_CERT=verify,
    the pilot groups first.
  • Not only Hyper-V: Proxmox VE, VMware vSphere, Veeam Backup & Replication and Citrix Virtual Apps and Desktops in
    the Health Center, each from its own API, read only; the hardware of HPE iLO, Lenovo XClarity and Supermicro too.
  • Peripherals and inventory: model, serial number, BIOS and the USB devices of each device; a required peripheral
    that is gone is shown to look at. Rules per group, an inventory to export, a status page in HTML.
  • Health Center: maintenance per item, notices when a problem comes or goes, history and forecast, the services
    as their users reach them, leftovers, a baseline to come back to, iDRAC (power, supplies, temperatures), custom
    checks and commands, backup of the VMs to a share (a Linux VM is copied with its memory), the witness and the FSMO
    roles, administration without administrator rights (JEA), and -HealthReport -Json for Task Scheduler.
  • The manager starts at once: its launcher draws the access window in a fraction of a second while the manager
    loads behind it; the menu and the titles come first, each page whole after. OpenThinRDP-Manager.cmd and .vbs go:
    start OpenThinRDP-Manager.exe (-Install for a shortcut on each PC).
  • The manager refuses changed files: the files of a release are signed (MANIFEST.txt, the project key); a file
    changed, missing or added stops the start with their list.
  • Remote screen with consent: REMOTE_CONSENT=ask asks the user of the device before the screen is shared.
  • Note: Microsoft Defender with old signatures may hold the manager back; update its signatures.

0.2.18 (2026-10-23)

  • Test first. A session whose UDP transport stalls comes back by itself: seen twice in a video call, the pointer
    moved but the picture, the camera and the microphone stayed stuck until the device was switched off, while the
    server sent nothing for hours. When FreeRDP keeps sending over UDP and nothing comes back for 60 seconds, the
    session is closed and reopened over TCP until the device restarts (TRANSPORT=auto); what FreeRDP was doing goes to
    session-hang.log. An idle session is never taken for one.
  • The log of the last session survives a power off: the FreeRDP log (in memory) is kept at shutdown as
    /var/log/openthinrdp/last-session.log. A device switched off during a session says so in its events, with the
    threads of FreeRDP in session-hang.log. Both, and a stalled UDP transport, are in the anonymous counts.
  • A reinstalled device: its new SSH key is confirmed from any command: Restart, Update and the other device
    commands now ask whether to accept the new host key, as the remote screen did, instead of showing the ssh warning.

0.2.9 (2026-10-21)

  • Groups of devices, as folders of the management folder. config\groups\<group>\_group.conf holds the settings
    of a group (between common.conf and each device); the files of its devices sit next to it, so the folder a file
    is in is the group of the device, and moving the file moves the device. Devices with different accounts, or the
    same one, in as many groups as you like. The manager shows the devices in collapsible groups, creates and deletes
    groups, and moves a device to another group (its file moves in the share).
  • The devices as a tree, as in the Health Center: the site (your domain), its groups and the devices, each with
    the dot of its state. Selecting the site shows the settings of every device (moved here from the Settings page),
    a group its own (what is ticked is set there and wins from there down, the rest shows the value inherited from
    the site), a device its own. Drag a device onto a group, or right click; New group above the tree.
  • Health Center settings in tabs (Hyper-V, domain and desktops, SQL and files, devices and backups, checks),
    in a window of fixed size with Save always in sight. The updates of the manager are on the Updates page.
  • Fewer false problems in the Health Center: a former broker still named as fallback by some devices is no
    longer shown as a broker failing every sign-in, nor as missing in the permissions of the hosts; the time source
    of a server is not judged before the domain controllers are read.
  • A renamed device just gets its new name: no empty file of its own is left behind, and it stays in its group.
  • A faster manager. The vault opens in 0.2 s instead of 2.4 s after the access code (the PBKDF2 of Windows,
    the same keys); its C# is compiled once and kept, so it loads in half the time; and while the Health Center reads
    the servers it no longer stops the window when its page is not open.
  • 0.2.8 (hardware test, package list) was not published on its own: it is part of this release, with the new ISO.

    Windows keeps the microphone of such a device in the console session: Teams and Zoom in the remote session found
    no microphone at all. Webcams and USB headsets now stay on the thin client: the camera goes through the RDP
    camera channel ([MS-RDPECAM]), the microphone and the speaker through the RDP audio channels. Devices plugged in
    during the session follow the same rule, and a webcam an earlier version had passed over USB is given back to
    the thin client by itself (its Linux drivers are attached again).

  • Updates installed during a session take effect when it is over: as soon as the device is back to the
    sign-in screen, or a new connection starts, the screen reloads with the new version (before: at the next start).

  • The mouse pointer on the kiosk screens (sign-in, progress, messages) hides while the mouse is still and comes
    back as soon as it moves; in the remote desktop it is Windows' own pointer as before.

  • Fix: a device moved to another management folder by MGMT_SHARE in the common settings went back to the
    folder of its image at the next report, and kept bouncing between the two.

0.1.3 (2026-10-12)

In production: this version runs a fleet of thin clients converted in place, updated from their management folder.

  • New screens for the user (GTK, full screen, on the background of the site): sign-in with the clock and date,
    information and settings small in the top right corner, power with Restart and Shut down bottom right,
    the web button bottom left; desktop start with a progress bar that follows the real steps (broker, redirection,
    desktop on screen); errors, no network, session open elsewhere, session taken over, end of session with a
    countdown. Settings, diagnostics and PIN stay simple dialogs (administrators only).
  • Software updates: a notice with the progress in the top right corner while one installs; Shut down
    during an update     shows it full screen and switches off when it is done (before, it interrupted the install).
  • The boot screen always shows the picture and colors of the image (OpenThinRDP's, or yours when you build your
    own image); the picture of the management folder is the background of the sign-in screen, which is drawn
    again as soon as a new one arrives. In the manager the button is now Logo and background....
  • Devices still showing the provisional logo of 0.1.0 get the current one (only exact project pictures are
    replaced, never those of your own image).
  • FreeRDP: the USB fix for a client hanging on disconnect is now FreeRDP#13679
    (by the FreeRDP maintainer, from our #13677); #13674 was approved.
  • Manager: a picture whose width already fit was not scaled to the height.
  • Day and month of the sign-in screen follow LANGUAGE, not the system locale.

0.1.2 (2026-10-12)

  • Fix: update packages always carry 755/644 modes. The 0.1.1 package was built with umask 002, and a device
    that installed it got a group-writable /etc, so sshd refused every key. Installing 0.1.2 repairs it.
    The 0.1.1 package was withdrawn; the 0.1.1 ISO was not affected.
  • The README links always point to the latest release.

0.1.1 (2026-10-12)

  • Security: no text consoles. Ctrl+Alt+F1..F12 and Ctrl+Alt+Backspace do nothing in the kiosk, no spare
    consoles with a login prompt, the kiosk account is locked. In 0.1.0 kiosk had no password: anybody at the
    keyboard could get a shell with Ctrl+Alt+F2. Devices updated from 0.1.0 are fixed too.
  • Secrets (Wi-Fi key, management folder password) are never printed back, only their hash.
  • Logo and boot image from the Windows manager, for every device or for one (config\branding\); the boot
    splash follows, also when only the colors change.
  • Diagnostics button (ℹ) on the sign-in form, without a PIN: network, brokers, time, management folder, events.
  • A new device names itself thinclient- + the last 6 digits of its MAC address.
  • Management folder account in the settings dialog and in the central settings (MGMT_USER, MGMT_PASSWORD).
  • MULTIMON=auto|off: one remote desktop over every monitor.
  • Certificate authorities of the site are trusted by the system too.
  • Settings labels with a colon (hh:mm, VID:PID) were cut short since 0.1.0.
  • README: direct connection to a Windows PC, what comes next.
