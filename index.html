OpenThinRDP: the open source thin client OS with RDP over UDP

Free thin client OS for Windows Remote Desktop, RDS and Hyper-V VDI that ships FreeRDP with the UDP transport, which upstream FreeRDP does not have yet: as far as we know, no other open source thin client OS offers RDP over UDP. Turn small PCs and old thin clients into kiosks that boot straight into a sign-in screen, with USB done right for VDI, central management from a shared folder and signed updates. No licence per device, no server, no cloud.

20 frames per second with half the packets lost, 18 with 70%, where TCP stalls at 20% (the measurements).

RDP over UDP, on a free thin client

Windows can send the remote desktop over UDP (MS-RDPEMT / MS-RDPEUDP): instead of queueing behind lost TCP packets, it adapts frame rate and quality to the line. Windows' own client has done it for years; on Linux, FreeRDP's UDP transport is still a pull request under review (FreeRDP #13608), so thin client systems built on upstream FreeRDP connect over TCP only. OpenThinRDP ships FreeRDP built with that transport, tested on real thin clients against a Windows 11 VDI, with automatic fallback to TCP.

On a bad line: OpenThinRDP over UDP against TCP

It started with a faulty switch port losing 43% of the large packets: over TCP the remote desktop froze. We reproduced that line on a real thin client, then raised the loss on every packet from the virtual machine step by step. Windows 11 VDI behind an RD Connection Broker, full-screen load that changes every pixel, frame rate and loss measured by the server.

* The other rows lose that share of every packet from the virtual machine; this one 43% of the large packets and 5% of the small ones, as on the faulty port. ** At 70% the TCP connection RDP keeps beside the UDP tunnel may end the session after 20 s without an acknowledgement. One environment (Dell OptiPlex 3000 Thin Client, Windows 11 VDI on Hyper-V), loss emulated with netem: the method and the scripts, to get your own numbers.

Getting there took a fix to FreeRDP's UDP transport, which we found and reported in #13608 and which is now part of it. Every step: docs/udp.md. What is in production and what is only tested: status.

10 hours of aggressive soak tests

Runs of 2, 5 and 3 hours on a real thin client: VM CPU at 100 %, 2 % and 10 % packet loss, 15 Mbit/s cap, 40 ms jitter, network cut for 20 s, client killed. UDP was never worse than TCP; the last 3-hour run, on the current build, was alive 178 of 178 minutes and never left UDP (5-hour run, 3-hour run).

Back on UDP after a cut

After a 20 second network cut the session comes back by itself, on UDP again, in about 12 seconds.

Your choice per device

TRANSPORT=auto (UDP, TCP when UDP is blocked), udp (UDP only) or tcp, from the device or centrally for the whole fleet.

What it does

RDP over UDP

FreeRDP 3 built with the UDP transport (MS-RDPEMT / RDPEUDP), still under review upstream: smoother sessions on busy servers, lossy Wi-Fi and VPNs. Automatic fallback to TCP, or force either one per device.

RDS, VDI or a single PC

Through an RD Connection Broker to session hosts or a Hyper-V VDI collection (load balance info, fallback brokers), or straight to any Windows PC with Remote Desktop on.

USB done right for VDI

Webcams and headsets work in meetings: camera through the RDP camera channel, microphone through the RDP audio channel, so Teams and Zoom in the session see both. Signature keys, tachograph keys and other special devices go to Windows as whole USB devices, also when plugged in during the session. Smart card readers through the smart card channel, touch screens as real touch.

Managed from a shared folder

No agent and no management server: devices write their status to an SMB share and read central settings, logo, background and updates from it. A Windows manager (English and Italian, no install) edits it all.

Signed updates

From GitHub releases or from your folder when you decide: installed in the background, applied as soon as the session is over. Debian security updates install by themselves; FreeRDP updates only with tested releases.

Locked down kiosk

Boots in seconds into the sign-in screen: no desktop, no menus, no text consoles. Settings behind a PIN, diagnostics for the help desk, power schedule and Wake-on-LAN.

Try it in five minutes

1. Download the ISO and write it to a USB stick (Rufus in DD mode, balenaEtcher or dd).
2. Boot the PC from the stick: the live system starts by itself after 10 seconds and never writes to the disk.
3. Set your broker (or the name of a Windows PC) and sign in.
4. Like it? Restart and choose Install: it shows the internal disk it is going to erase and asks before writing anything, then about three minutes. Never a USB disk. For rollouts, Unattended install skips the question.

Needs a 64-bit x86 PC or thin client with UEFI (Secure Boot works), 2 GB of RAM and 8 GB of disk.

In production

OpenThinRDP runs the whole thin client fleet of the company it comes from, every day: 14 Dell OptiPlex 3000 Thin Clients at desks, tills, a laboratory and weighing stations, on a Windows 11 VDI on Hyper-V behind two RD Connection Brokers. The devices were converted in place from their previous system and take every release as a signed package from their management folder, installed during sessions and applied when the session is over. FreeRDP security releases reach OpenThinRDP within 7 days (3.32.2: four hours). What is in production, what is only tested and what does not exist yet: status. Reports from other hardware are very welcome in the discussions.

How it compares

OpenThinRDP is for networks built on Windows Remote Desktop that want a free, open client which is simple to manage. If you need Citrix or Horizon, the commercial systems are the right choice. Detailed comparison with IGEL OS, Dell ThinOS, Stratodesk NoTouch, ThinStation and openthinclient.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes: Apache 2.0, no licence per device, no management server, no cloud account.

Which hardware does it run on?

x86-64 PCs and thin clients with UEFI. The reference device is the Dell OptiPlex 3000 Thin Client; other models (HP t-series, Wyse, Fujitsu, small office PCs) should work and reports are welcome. ARM devices are not supported yet.

Does it support Citrix or VMware Horizon?

No, RDP only: Microsoft Remote Desktop Services (RD Connection Broker, session hosts), Hyper-V VDI collections, or a single Windows PC or server.

How does RDP over UDP work here?

The image ships FreeRDP built from the pull request that adds the UDP transport (FreeRDP #13608) plus a fix of ours that keeps it going on lossy lines (19-21 fps with 50% of the packets lost). With TRANSPORT=auto it uses UDP and falls back to TCP when UDP is blocked.

Can I put my company logo on it?

Yes: from the manager, for every device or for one, or built into your own image.

What comes next?

VPN (WireGuard, OpenVPN), RD Gateway, 802.1X, network installation (PXE), more tested hardware and languages. Tell us what you need in the discussions.
