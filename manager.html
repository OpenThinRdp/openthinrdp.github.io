Know what is broken before your users call

Your whole infrastructure, checked in 30 seconds. Free. OpenThinRDP Manager is the health center small and mid-size IT teams were missing: point it at your Hyper-V cluster, Proxmox, vSphere, Remote Desktop farm, Citrix site, domain, SQL Server and Veeam, and it shows you every problem, the worst first, each one in a plain sentence with what it means and the fix.

No agent to install. No server to build. No per-host license. Download, extract, run.

The calls it saves you

  • "Nobody can sign in." It shows who cannot sign in right now, and why: the broker refusing, the VM in saved state, the desktop that will not start. Then the repair, one click, after you read it.
  • "The cluster went down when we patched one host." It tells you before: the witness is offline, one host cannot carry the VMs of the other, a CSV is almost full.
  • "The backup failed last week and nobody noticed." Failed Veeam jobs, Proxmox backups that did not run, VMs whose Hyper-V backups are only crash-consistent: on the first page, every morning.
  • "The certificate expired on Saturday." Every certificate of every server, what uses it, when it expires, whether it renews by itself; your CA and its revocation list.
  • "A disk died and the RAID was degraded for a month." What the iDRAC says of every part: each disk with its remaining life and a failure it predicts, RAID and its battery, memory module by module, power supplies, fans, temperatures, the hardware log and every firmware version.

Why IT teams pick it

  • Free for any use, also commercial. No trial, no account, no "per socket".
  • Nothing to install on your servers. It reads them over WinRM, REST and SQL with an account you choose; read only, unless you confirm a repair.
  • You can read every command it runs on your servers: they ship as plain PowerShell next to it.
  • Fixes, not only alarms. Restart the stuck service, bring the role online, sync the clock, resume the database, back up the VMs to a share: each one shown before it runs.
  • It answers in sentences. "The witness of VDICL is offline: the share \\nas1\witness refuses the computer account" instead of a red dot.
  • Born in production. Built by sysadmins for a real site with two Hyper-V hosts, Remote Desktop and SQL AlwaysOn, and running there every day.
  • It grows fast. Proxmox, vSphere, Veeam, Citrix and the enterprise CA arrived in a single release; what users ask for comes next.

Up and running in five minutes

  1. Download the zip and extract it.
  2. Start OpenThinRDP-Manager.exe, choose The servers alone.
  3. Settings > Health Center: your hosts, brokers, SQL listener, Proxmox or vCenter. Done: the Home fills in.

Questions

Is it a PowerShell script that edits configuration files?

No. That was the first manager of OpenThinRDP, in its first weeks (up to 0.2.22). Today it is a Windows application with releases of its own (1.0.0 from 28 October 2026), signed with the project key, that updates itself; the health center works also without any thin client. The commands it runs on your servers stay plain PowerShell you can read.

Is it really free?

Yes, for any use, also in a company and for your customers. It is not open source: its license forbids selling it or building a competing product with it.

Does it need an agent or a server?

No. It runs on your PC and reads your servers with Windows tools (WinRM, SQL) and the REST APIs of Proxmox, vCenter and Veeam. Nothing is installed on them.

Can it break something?

It only reads, until you choose a repair; every repair shows what it will do and asks first. What it runs on servers is plain PowerShell you can read.

I run Proxmox, vSphere, Veeam or Citrix: can I use it?

Yes: they arrived in this release, built on their official APIs. Be one of the first sites to run it, and tell us what it found: reports from real sites are what we work on first.

What is OpenThinRDP?

The same team builds OpenThinRDP, a free open source thin client OS for Remote Desktop on any hypervisor (RDP over UDP, Secure Boot, signed updates). With it, the manager also runs your whole fleet of thin clients from the same window.
