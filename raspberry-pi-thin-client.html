Raspberry Pi as a Remote Desktop thin client

A Raspberry Pi 4 costs little, uses a few watts and is silent: a natural terminal for a Windows desktop. OpenThinRDP builds as an SD card image for it, the same system as on a PC: the Windows sign-in at power on, RDP over UDP, sound, USB and the same central management.

Status, said plainly: experimental. The image is built and tested in emulation (QEMU), not yet on a real board. If you have a Pi 4 and an hour, your report is what moves it forward.

What works and what to check

  • Boards: Raspberry Pi 4 Model B with 2 GB or more, the Pi 400 and the CM4. The Pi 5 is not supported yet.
  • The same system as on a PC: kiosk, settings dialog, management folder, signed updates, FreeRDP with UDP, on Debian 13 arm64 with Debian's own kernel.
  • No installer: the image is written to an SD card or a USB disk and boots as an installed system.
  • Browsers: Chromium, Firefox or Brave for the web page buttons; Chrome and Edge do not exist for ARM Linux.
  • To check on a real board: video output on both HDMI ports, sound over HDMI and USB, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, and smoothness of video in the session. The list: arm.md.

Try it

  1. Download the image from the pre-release.
  2. Write it to an SD card with Raspberry Pi Imager (Use custom), balenaEtcher or dd.
  3. Start the Pi, set your Windows PC or your RD Connection Broker, sign in.
  4. Tell us what worked in the discussions.

A Pi or a used thin client?

Raspberry Pi 4 Used x86 thin client (Wyse 5070, HP t630, Futro S740)
Status with OpenThinRDP experimental supported: x86 is what runs in production
Price the board plus power supply, case and SD card often less, with case, power supply and storage included
Storage SD card (slower, wears out) or USB disk eMMC or M.2
Video decoding in the session to be checked proven on x86

For an office today, a used x86 thin client is the safer choice (which one). For a test at home, or to help the project, the Pi is welcome.
